The Seven Open Axioms

Grounded mechanical principles paired with immediate daily somatic practices.

Nothing passing through conscious awareness belongs to the observer: thoughts, bodily sensations, achievements, or material artifacts are transient visitors. Suffering is the thermodynamic friction of holding onto that which is naturally flowing.

Practice: When experiencing physical or mental friction, mentally open your hands and whisper: "This too is passing through."

Between impulse and action lies an infinitesimal threshold. Whoever masters this micro-interval escapes deterministic programming and enters sovereign moral choice.

Practice: Count two full respiratory cycles before responding to any communication that triggers defensive arousal.

No intermediary possesses private access to reality. Any entity, teacher, or hierarchy demanding submission or money to mediate truth is an extractive bottleneck.

Practice: Consult direct natural observation and repeatable experiments over revered human authorities.

Cruelty contracts the nervous system; generosity expands it. Ethical integrity is not an ancient obligation but an immediate physiological hygiene.

Practice: Perform one unannounced, uncredited act of physical assistance within your immediate neighborhood today.

The ego is a process, not a solid monument. Treating opinions and self-narratives as immutable structures produces fragility when circumstances inevitably evolve.

Practice: Identify one cherished conviction each week and write down two valid counter-arguments with sincere intellectual respect.

A diagram points to water; it cannot quench thirst. This web protocol succeeds only when it prompts the participant to close the screen and engage tangible reality.

Practice: Step outside for twenty minutes daily with empty hands, observing sensory details without digital capture.

Truth cannot be held in a private vault. Every node that receives insight is obligated to mirror and distribute it freely, without modifying core principles for personal vanity.

Practice: Share tools, knowledge, and quiet assistance anonymously with those building local resilience.
